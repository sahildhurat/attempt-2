Primary research interviews

Seven interviews, conducted September–October 2026 against a 22-question guide: photo habits and remembered clues, then a past retrieval the participant had not resolved, then an observed attempt to find it.

Interviews: 7
Guide: 22 questions
Period: Sep–Oct 2026

Interview 1 — Nikhil

28 years old | Mumbai | Operations analyst

Android user; Google Photos is his primary library; about six years of use and roughly 14,000 items.

Past retrieval scenario: A cafe photo from a Hampi trip roughly two years earlier, sought in September 2026 to recommend the place to a colleague.

past outcome: Stopped without finding the intended photo; its availability in his library remains unconfirmed.

Photo habits and remembered clues

Q1 What kinds of images end up in Google Photos, and how do you use them afterward?

Mostly backup. I take everything on my phone and leave it there. I'll send a few pictures to the trip group, but I don't make albums unless somebody asks. My screenshots are mixed in with everything else.

Q2 Tell me about the most recent time you tried to find one particular older image but did not have clear details to search with.

A couple of weeks ago I wanted a photo of this little cafe in Hampi. We went there on a scooter, I think. I could picture the place, but the name was completely gone.

Q3 What prompted you to look for it at that moment, and what did you need the image for?

A colleague was asking for Hampi food recommendations. I wanted to send that picture because it had the place in the background. Another cafe photo wouldn't really help him go to the same place.

Q4 Before you opened any app that day, how would you have described the image to another person?

Small place, yellowish wall, blue chairs, and Dev sitting with a cold coffee. We had parked the scooter near some steps. That's what comes into my head, not a name or a date.

Q5 Which parts of that description felt certain, and which were guesses or details you could not recall?

Dev and the coffee I'm quite sure about. The chairs might have been green, honestly. I first thought we went in 2025, but now I think it was the trip before that.

Q6 What made you believe the image was saved in Google Photos?

I remember taking it on my phone and showing him because he looked half asleep. My camera photos normally back up. I haven't actually seen this particular one in Photos since then, though.

Q7 What was the first thing you did to find it, and why did you start there?

I typed 'Hampi cafe'. I thought that would at least get me to the trip. I wouldn't normally type the whole story about the scooter and Dev being sleepy.

Q8 What did you expect that first step to show you, and what appeared instead?

I expected a small set of cafe pictures. I got food and restaurant pictures, but I couldn't identify this one. A couple looked promising until I opened them and saw they were somewhere else.

Past outcome and observed retrieval task

Q9 Walk me through what happened next, step by step, until you found it or stopped.

Then 'blue chairs', which wasn't useful. I went back to Hampi pictures and started scrolling. I also jumped to a part of 2025 because I thought I knew the year. After maybe eight minutes I left it.

Q10 How did that attempt end, and what did you do about the original need afterward?

I sent him two other places we'd been to. So I answered his message, but I didn't find the photo I wanted. It wasn't worth keeping him waiting while I searched my phone.

Q11 Choose an image you genuinely want to find and have not already located for this session. Before opening Google Photos, tell me what you remember and how you would know you had found the right one.

Can I try that cafe again? I still haven't found it. Dev said later that it might have been near a temple, but he's guessing too. I'd recognize him sitting there with the wall behind him.

Q12 Looking at this screen, what makes you decide which results or images to inspect?

I'm looking for the wall color first. There are loads of temple pictures here. I'll open anything that looks like an outdoor table, but I keep going past the selfies.

Q13 What tells you this is, or is not, the particular image you had in mind?

This one has blue chairs, but it's a big restaurant and the wall is white. I don't think it's right. Although now I'm second-guessing the wall color as well.

Q14 What do you plan to try next, and what led you to that choice?

I'll remove 'chairs' and just try 'Hampi' again. Maybe the chair thing is throwing me off. If I can find pictures of the scooter day, I might recognize what came before it.

Q15 At this point, would you keep looking or stop if you were on your own? What drives that decision?

I'd stop now, if I'm honest. I'm opening places that aren't it, and I'm not sure I'm even in the right trip. If Dev replies with the name, I'll have another go.

Workarounds and comparisons

Q16 What, if anything, did you try outside Google Photos to complete the task?

After the first attempt I messaged Dev: 'What was that cafe with the blue chairs?' He said maybe near a temple. I checked the trip chat too, but I didn't go through every photo in it.

Q17 What did that workaround give you that you did not have before?

A possible area, that's all. Not a name, and no picture. His reply made me less sure about my version because he remembered different chairs. We both remember enjoying it, though.

Q18 Tell me about another recent time you found an older image relatively easily, even though you did not start with all the details.

There was a paragliding picture from another trip. I didn't know the date, but I tried 'paragliding' and recognized the helmet straightaway. That took almost no effort.

Q19 How did that attempt differ from the one we discussed earlier?

Paragliding is something I can actually name. The cafe is just a scene in my head. Also, I probably have five paragliding pictures and hundreds of photos of sitting around eating.

Q20 What other attempts like this have you made in the last month or two, and which had the biggest consequence for you?

Maybe three attempts recently. A waterfall one, a hotel balcony, and this cafe. The cafe mattered more because somebody wanted to visit it. The others were just me wanting to show someone a nice picture or post to my Insta.

Q21 Were there details you remembered but chose not to enter, or things you chose not to try? What influenced that choice?

I didn't type 'the cafe we went to after renting a scooter'. That sounds like something I'd say to Dev, not put into a search box. I also avoided entering a year once I wasn't sure.

Q22 What about your experience of finding that image have we not covered?

I say I know the photo exists, but I suppose I really know I took it. Those aren't quite the same thing. Usually I only start doubting that after I've wasted a bit of time.

Interview 2 — Snehal

31 years old | Pune | HR professional

Android user; Google Photos is her primary library; about five years of use and roughly 21,000 items.

Past retrieval scenario: A phone photo of her mother's prescription from a September 2025 appointment, sought in September 2026 to show the document at a follow-up appointment.

past outcome: Found the intended prescription after using an appointment message to locate the relevant day and opening several similar document photos.

Photo habits and remembered clues

Q1 What kinds of images end up in Google Photos, and how do you use them afterward?

Camera photos, family pictures, bills, and screenshots. If someone hands me a paper I might need again, I photograph it. Photos is usually the first place I check; I don't have a proper folder for documents.

Q2 Tell me about the most recent time you tried to find one particular older image but did not have clear details to search with.

Last month I wanted Mum's old prescription. I remembered a blue clinic stamp and that the paper was folded. I thought it was from winter, but I couldn't remember which appointment.

Q3 What prompted you to look for it at that moment, and what did you need the image for?

We were going for a follow-up and wanted to show what had been written last time. A photograph of any prescription wouldn't help. I needed that particular document, with the date and handwriting visible.

Q4 Before you opened any app that day, how would you have described the image to another person?

A white sheet on our dining table, folded across the middle, with a blue stamp near the bottom. I can picture Mum's hand holding the edge. I can't picture the medicine names clearly enough to type them.

Q5 Which parts of that description felt certain, and which were guesses or details you could not recall?

The fold and blue stamp felt clear. I was guessing the season. I had mixed up the appointment with another time she was unwell, and I didn't remember the exact date or the words on the page.

Q6 What made you believe the image was saved in Google Photos?

I took the photo on my phone and remember sending it to my sister afterward. I normally back up my camera pictures. That made me fairly confident, although I hadn't looked at this one in Photos recently.

Q7 What was the first thing you did to find it, and why did you start there?

I searched 'prescription'. I thought that would be quicker than scrolling through a year. I didn't search for a medicine name because I couldn't remember one accurately.

Q8 What did you expect that first step to show you, and what appeared instead?

I saw lots of white pages and some medicine-box photos. Several had blue marks. In the little previews, I couldn't tell which document was Mum's or whether it was the older prescription.

Past outcome and observed retrieval task

Q9 Walk me through what happened next, step by step, until you found it or stopped.

I opened a few and checked the dates. Then I looked at the clinic's appointment message in WhatsApp. It was September 2025, not winter. I browsed that day in Photos and opened the folded sheet.

Q10 How did that attempt end, and what did you do about the original need afterward?

Found it, after maybe fifteen minutes with interruptions. I sent my sister a clearer crop. The handwriting and date matched what we needed to show, so I knew it was the right document.

Q11 Choose an image you genuinely want to find and have not already located for this session. Before opening Google Photos, tell me what you remember and how you would know you had found the right one.

There's a photo of our refrigerator warranty receipt I haven't found. I remember the shop name at the top and a staple shadow. I need it because the door seal is giving us trouble.

Q12 Looking at this screen, what makes you decide which results or images to inspect?

I'm looking at bill-like pages. I'll open the ones with the shop logo, because I can't read the item description from these previews. The page color by itself isn't much help.

Q13 What tells you this is, or is not, the particular image you had in mind?

This is the same shop, but it's a washing-machine bill. The layout fooled me until I opened it. The refrigerator receipt should have the model description and that staple near the corner.

Q14 What do you plan to try next, and what led you to that choice?

I'll try the shop name and check the appliance details on the promising ones. I don't remember the purchase month, so I'm leaving the year out rather than narrowing it wrongly again.

Q15 At this point, would you keep looking or stop if you were on your own? What drives that decision?

I'd keep going briefly. Oh, this says refrigerator and has the staple mark. I'll open the whole page and check the model. Yes, that's the receipt I meant.

Workarounds and comparisons

Q16 What, if anything, did you try outside Google Photos to complete the task?

For the prescription, first I looked for the file, but we had lost it. I opened the clinic chat and asked my sister whether she remembered when that visit was. I didn't ask her to tell me the medicine names from memory.

Q17 What did that workaround give you that you did not have before?

A date I trusted. It got me to a small stretch of the library. It didn't give me the photograph itself; I still had to inspect the documents there.

Q18 Tell me about another recent time you found an older image relatively easily, even though you did not start with all the details.

A screenshot of an online delivery confirmation. I remembered the shop name, searched for it, and recognized the orange header. There were only a couple of pictures that looked like it.

Q19 How did that attempt differ from the one we discussed earlier?

The order screenshot had a word I knew. For the prescription, I remembered the look of the paper but not its text. And blue stamps aren't very distinctive when you have several medical documents.

Q20 What other attempts like this have you made in the last month or two, and which had the biggest consequence for you?

Three or four recently: the prescription, a bill, and an old school circular for my niece. The prescription mattered most because we wanted the actual previous note at the appointment.

Q21 Were there details you remembered but chose not to enter, or things you chose not to try? What influenced that choice?

I didn't enter a medicine name I half-remembered. I also didn't type 'Mum' because she isn't actually in the picture apart from her hand. I wasn't sure that would identify the page.

Q22 What about your experience of finding that image have we not covered?

I say I remember the prescription, but I mostly remember how the paper looked. Finding a document category isn't the same as finding the right dated document inside it.

Interview 3 — Rohan

26 years old | Mumbai | Software engineer

Android user; Google Photos is his primary library; about seven years of use and roughly 27,000 items.

Past retrieval scenario: A photo from a trek to Garbett Plateau in 2017.

Past outcome: Did not find it in Google Photos. A friend found that photo in their shared google drive of the same trip

Photo habits and remembered clues

1. What kinds of images end up in Google Photos, and how do you use them afterward?

“Photos is my main storage place. I’ve used it for about seven years. Camera pictures, screenshots, tickets, bills—everything gets mixed together. I’ll save pictures friends send me too. I travel and trek a lot too, so all those photos are saved too

2. Tell me about the most recent time you tried to find one particular older image but did not have clear details to search with.

“Last month I wanted a photo from an old trek to Garbett Plateau in Matheran. We’re walking along this misty trail, and I’m at the back in a blue poncho. I remembered the scene really clearly, but I couldn’t place the year or month.”

3. What prompted you to look for it at that moment, and what did you need the image for?

“A friend was talking about going there, and our group started joking about how drenched we’d been. I wanted to send that particular picture of us walking in the rain. It was an awesome moment. A nice landscape shot wouldn’t show the moment we were talking about.”

4. Before you opened any app that day, how would you have described the image to another person?

“A muddy path with grass on either side, barely any view because of the mist. Amit is ahead of me with an orange backpack. I’m partly cut off at the edge in that ridiculous blue poncho. We’re facing away from the camera.”

5. Which parts of that description felt certain, and which were guesses or details you could not recall?

“The poncho and Amit’s bag felt definite. I wasn’t sure whether it was 2019 or 2021, I have done that trek thrice now. I don’t know whether the photo was near the top or earlier on the climb. I also couldn’t remember who took it. I remembered the joke better than those details.”

6. What made you believe the image was saved in Google Photos?

“I remember saving a batch of pictures Amit sent after the trek. I think this one appeared in a Photos memory later, too. That made me pretty confident, but I’d changed phones since then and hadn’t checked this particular image recently.”

7. What was the first thing you did to find it, and why did you start there?

“I typed ‘Garbett’. I knew the trek name, so that seemed like the obvious place to start. I didn’t think about whether that name would actually be attached to the photograph.”

8. What did you expect that first step to show you, and what appeared instead?

“I expected pictures from that outing, or at least a few mountain photos. Nothing I recognized came up for ‘Garbett’. I wasn’t sure whether the photo wasn’t there or whether that was just a useless search word for it.”

9. Walk me through what happened next, step by step, until you found it or stopped.

“I tried ‘Matheran’, which showed another visit and phtos from all 3 garbett treks, but not the one I wanted, then ‘trek’, which mixed together several outings. ‘Raincoat’ gave me some rainy-day pictures, but not this one. Then I scrolled around the rainy season in 2019. After about fifteen minutes I gave up for the time being.”

10. How did that attempt end, and what did you do about the original need afterward?

“I told the group I’d dig it out later and left the conversation without sending it. I hadn’t found the photo. It wasn’t urgent enough to keep everyone waiting, but it bothered me because I could almost see the thing in my head. My friend found it later”

11. Choose an image you genuinely want to find and have not already located for this session. Before opening Google Photos, tell me what you remember and how you would know you had found the right one.

“Can I try that trek picture again? Amit found the photo in his own library. He says August 2021. I still haven’t found the photo in mine, so we can try it. I’d recognize the blue poncho at the edge and his orange bag ahead of me.”

12. Looking at this screen, what makes you decide which results or images to inspect?

“I’m browsing around that date now. I’ll open the foggy trail pictures, even when I can’t see everyone properly in the little preview. I’m skipping the clear viewpoints. Those are the same trek, but they don’t look like the scene I remember.”

13. What tells you this is, or is not, the particular image you had in mind?

“This one has me in the blue poncho, but we’re standing still and facing the camera. That’s not it. In the one I want, we’re walking away, and you can hardly see my face. Just getting the clothes right isn’t enough.”

14. What do you plan to try next, and what led you to that choice?

“That lunch photo has reminded me we took the walking picture before we stopped to eat. I’ll check the pictures just before it. I’d been looking near the photos from the top, but I think this happened earlier.”

15. At this point, would you keep looking or stop if you were on your own? What drives that decision?

“I’d keep going now because we’ve narrowed it down. Oh, this looks right. Let me open it. Yes—Amit’s orange bag, the muddy path, and half my poncho cut off at the side. That’s the one. I’d send it and stop looking.”

16. What, if anything, did you try outside Google Photos to complete the task?

“After the first attempt, I messaged Amit asking when we’d gone. I also checked our old WhatsApp group, but the chat had years of messages. He found the picture.”

17. What did that workaround give you that you did not have before?

“It gave me the photo itself, and the confirmed date .”

18. Tell me about another recent time you found an older image relatively easily, even though you did not start with all the details.

“A photo of the serial-number sticker on my router. I couldn’t remember when I’d taken it, but I searched ‘router’ and there were only a few pictures. I opened the close-up with the sticker and found it almost immediately.”

19. How did that attempt differ from the one we discussed earlier?

“The router was something visible that I could name, and I didn’t have hundreds of similar photos. With the trek, I remembered the outing’s name and what we were doing. Lots of green, misty pictures looked nearly the same until I opened them.”

20. What other attempts like this have you made in the last month or two, and which had the biggest consequence for you?

“There was an old payment screenshot, an appliance receipt, and this trek photo. The receipt mattered more because I needed it to arrange a repair. The trek picture was annoying to lose, but I could leave it for another day.”

21. Were there details you remembered but chose not to enter, or things you chose not to try? What influenced that choice?

“I didn’t type ‘the trek where we all got soaked and Amit kept slipping’. That’s what I remembered, but it felt strange as a search. I also avoided searching Amit’s name because he’s facing away in the photo and I haven’t named his face in Photos.”

22. What about your experience of finding that image have we not covered?

“Once I found the right day’s photos, I still had to find the right moment. We’d worn the same things all day. I think that’s why I got impatient: every picture looked close, but none was the one I wanted.”

Interview 4 — Prajakta

35 years old | Navi Mumbai | Architect

Android user; Google Photos is her primary library; about eight years of use and roughly 32,000 items.

Past retrieval scenario: A candid balcony photo from a Naggar holiday in February 2023, sought in August 2026 for an anniversary print.

past outcome: Found the intended frame after opening and comparing many similar photos from the right scene.

Photo habits and remembered clues

Q1 What kinds of images end up in Google Photos, and how do you use them afterward?

Camera pictures, bills, screenshots, and things I save for reference. I take too many versions of the same shot. Later I favorite the sharp, tidy ones. The less polished pictures stay buried with everything else.

Q2 Tell me about the most recent time you tried to find one particular older image but did not have clear details to search with.

In August I was finding a balcony picture from a holiday near Manali. We were in Naggar, actually. It's the one where I'm laughing with a mug halfway up, rather than smiling for the camera.

Q3 What prompted you to look for it at that moment, and what did you need the image for?

I wanted to print it for our anniversary. We have prettier photographs from that holiday, but this one feels like us. It was one particular expression I wanted, not just a photo on a balcony.

Q4 Before you opened any app that day, how would you have described the image to another person?

Me in a brown sweater, a mug near my face, mountain mist behind me. My partner said something ridiculous and I was laughing with my eyes almost closed. He kept taking pictures through the whole thing.

Q5 Which parts of that description felt certain, and which were guesses or details you could not recall?

I knew the sweater, the mug, and the morning light. I wasn't sure of the cottage name or date. I couldn't describe exactly which split-second expression it was, but I knew I'd recognize it.

Q6 What made you believe the image was saved in Google Photos?

We'd transferred those photos to my phone after the trip, and I'd seen them in Photos. I remembered rejecting that one for posting because my hair was everywhere. So I was fairly sure it was there.

Q7 What was the first thing you did to find it, and why did you start there?

'Balcony', then 'Manali'. I thought the scenery would get me to the right set. I wasn't expecting either word to pick the exact expression for me.

Q8 What did you expect that first step to show you, and what appeared instead?

The balcony search showed several holidays. Manali got me closer and I recognized the cottage. The tricky part came afterward: there were so many almost identical pictures of that morning.

Past outcome and observed retrieval task

Q9 Walk me through what happened next, step by step, until you found it or stopped.

I opened one, swiped, went back, opened another. I must have looked at twenty or thirty frames. Eventually I saw the one with the mug tilted and my eyes closed. Maybe fifteen minutes, most of it comparing.

Q10 How did that attempt end, and what did you do about the original need afterward?

Found it and sent it for printing. It was worth the effort for that occasion. Normally I probably would have chosen a nearby frame and pretended that was the one I had in mind.

Q11 Choose an image you genuinely want to find and have not already located for this session. Before opening Google Photos, tell me what you remember and how you would know you had found the right one.

There's one from the same trip where we're walking back after visiting a monastery. It was windy and my scarf was across my mouth. The monastery isn't actually in the picture. I'm not sure which day.

Q12 Looking at this screen, what makes you decide which results or images to inspect?

I'm looking at monastery pictures to establish the day, not because I think the walking photo will match that word. This building looks right. I'll open it and check when it was taken.

Q13 What tells you this is, or is not, the particular image you had in mind?

This walking picture is close, but my scarf is down and he's looking at the camera. In the one I remember, he's halfway out of frame and the scarf is flying. Let me open it rather than guess from here.

Q14 What do you plan to try next, and what led you to that choice?

I'll go through the photos after that building picture. We walked back straight afterward. The windy one ought to be in that stretch. Yes, this is it - he's nearly cut off at the edge.

Q15 At this point, would you keep looking or stop if you were on your own? What drives that decision?

I'd keep looking for a little while because I was in the right day. Now I've found it, I'd stop. If it were just a casual share, I'd have been happy with another walking picture much earlier.

Workarounds and comparisons

Q16 What, if anything, did you try outside Google Photos to complete the task?

I asked my partner which month that holiday was. He said February, so that helped me check the set. I didn't ask him to find the exact laughing frame; he wouldn't know which one I meant.

Q17 What did that workaround give you that you did not have before?

The month, and confirmation that I was thinking of the right holiday. It didn't solve the expression problem. I still had to inspect the pictures myself.

Q18 Tell me about another recent time you found an older image relatively easily, even though you did not start with all the details.

A photo from a trek where I was standing next to a very bright yellow sign. I searched trek pictures and recognized the sign. There wasn't a run of thirty versions of that one moment.

Q19 How did that attempt differ from the one we discussed earlier?

For the sign photo, the difference was visible even when the picture was small. With the balcony pictures, every thumbnail looked like brown sweater, mug, mountains. The detail I cared about only showed when I opened it.

Q20 What other attempts like this have you made in the last month or two, and which had the biggest consequence for you?

I looked for a few because of the anniversary. This one mattered most. I also wanted a beach picture and just chose another when I couldn't find the original. I don't always have this much patience.

Q21 Were there details you remembered but chose not to enter, or things you chose not to try? What influenced that choice?

I didn't search 'happy' or 'laughing'. Half that trip would fit. I thought about 'eyes closed', but it sounded oddly specific and I wasn't convinced it would work. So I carried on opening frames.

Q22 What about your experience of finding that image have we not covered?

Finding the right place isn't always finding the right photo. I sometimes have the correct set in front of me and still spend ages. Also, I favorite the polished pictures, then later want the messy ones.
