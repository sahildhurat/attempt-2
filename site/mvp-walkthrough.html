The MVP walkthrough

Every part of the running build, end to end: speak or type a fragment of what you remember, compare the three search modes against each other, ask the person who was there, scratch the daily card, and watch a phrase you saved come back as the thing that finds the photo. No sound — captions are embedded, and the transcript is below.

Length: 7 min 43 s
Demo library: 37 items
Modes compared: 3
Sign-in: none

Screen capture of the live build. Voice input uses the browser’s speech API, so that part needs Chrome — Brave does not support the flow. Everything else works anywhere.

What the walkthrough covers

  1. Four places to start — Library browses, Find searches from remembered fragments, Card invites a memory note, Index shows the phrases already saved against photos.
  2. Speaking instead of typing — voice search, and a spoken note on the daily card. The recognised words appear as editable text before they are sent, so a misheard word can be corrected rather than silently searched.
  3. What is in the library — 37 items spanning 2019–2024: 30 photos, five receipts and two screenshots, with type badges so documents are findable in the grid.
  4. Describing a photo in your own words — a café with yellow walls and green chairs; the build returns candidates and asks a question that distinguishes them, rather than guessing.
  5. Uncertainty shown rather than hidden — near-identical frames are grouped as the same moment, and the build says plainly that it cannot tell them apart from what it has.
  6. Three modes side by side — Classic keyword search, a single confident answer, and The One Where. Classic returns 21 items for “cafe” and nothing at all for the longer vague description; the single answer picks one photo and explains itself.
  7. An honest no-match — asked for a clinic receipt that is not in the library, the build says so instead of offering the nearest thing.
  8. Pass It On — a two-device simulation: your search on the left, the other person’s reply on the right. It asks about the occasion, keeps both recollections when they disagree, and files the reply as a deposit.
  9. The scratch card — reveal a forgotten photo, skip it freely, write or speak a line, then search that line and watch the photo come back badged YOUR WORDS.
  10. Your own photos — the upload notice, the picker, indexing, and a search across what you added. This run used ten images from the public demo library rather than private photos.

What the walkthrough does not show

The capture states its own gaps rather than editing around them: the API-limit wait during indexing is shortened; uploaded images without EXIF dates were grouped as a same-day set and the site warns about it on screen; Reset demo state and Delete everything are shown but were not executed, so their outcomes are not demonstrated; and on the classic-mode no-match, “Try describing it instead” was visible but clicking it did not visibly change the mode in that run.
